Forklift Certification in Scarborough: What It Costs, How Long It Takes, What You Get

A plain look at forklift certification in Scarborough: the listed prices, how long people report it taking, and what the card does and does not mean.

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Forklift Certification Scarborough

By A.RSM Training Centre · September 28, 2026 · 6 min read

If you are searching for forklift certification in Scarborough, you probably want three answers before you pick up the phone: what does it cost, how long will it take, and what do I walk away with. Those are fair questions, and a lot of schools in the GTA make you call to find out. Here is the straight version, including the parts nobody can promise.

What forklift certification in Scarborough costs

A.RSM Training Centre lists its lift-truck courses at $150 each: counterbalance forklift, reach truck, order picker and walkie rider. The skid-steer course is $350, the aerial work platform and scissor lift course is $200, fall protection is $100, and WHMIS 2015 and TDG are $49 each. The training centre is at 60 Nugget Ave in Scarborough.

There is one thing to check when you book. Some of the course pages on the school's older website mention an additional $75 for practical training and for creating and printing the wallet card. We have not confirmed that this applies to every course, so ask when you call and get the full number in one sentence: course, practical, card, total. Any reputable school will answer that without hesitation.

Ask for the all-in price before you book: course fee, practical evaluation, and wallet card. A low headline number that grows at the counter is the most common complaint about training in any city.

For context, other GTA providers publish forklift prices anywhere from roughly $50 for a theory-only offering to close to $200 for a full course. The cheap end is usually theory without a machine. That matters, and we will get to why.

How long does forklift training take?

The school has not published a fixed course length, and we are not going to invent one. What we can tell you is what students say. In Google reviews, several people report finishing and leaving with their certificate the same day, with comments along the lines of “under 3 hours” and “2 hours.” Another reviewer describes learning on a Raymond reach truck over two days. Treat those as individual experiences, not a schedule. Your time will depend on the type of truck, your experience, and how many people are in the group.

A useful way to think about it: the theory portion covers how a lift truck works and what can go wrong, and the practical portion is time on the machine. Someone who has driven a forklift for years and needs a current certificate will move faster than someone who has never sat on one. Neither should be rushed through the practical.

What you actually get

You get a training record and a wallet card that shows you were instructed and evaluated on a specific type of truck. That is useful, and employers in Scarborough, Markham and across the east end look for it. But it helps to understand what it is and what it is not.

Ontario's Regulation 851 (Industrial Establishments) does not use the word “forklift” and does not create a government forklift licence. It says lifting devices and mobile equipment must be operated by a competent person, or by a worker who is being instructed and accompanied by one. Under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, the employer must provide information, instruction and supervision. The Ministry's guidance on powered lift trucks says the employer establishes operator competence and may issue certificates. So the card from a training school is evidence of training, and the employer still has to be satisfied the person is competent on their equipment, in their building.

  • A record of theory and practical training on the truck type you took (counterbalance, reach truck, order picker or walkie rider).
  • A wallet card you can show a hiring manager or a supervisor.
  • A basis for the employer's own site orientation. Your new workplace still has to show you its aisles, racking, pedestrian routes and rules.

Which course do you need?

This trips up a lot of people. A counterbalance certificate does not automatically cover a reach truck. The controls, the mast, the way the load sits, and the aisle work are different. If the job posting says narrow aisle or racking to height, you probably want the reach truck course. If it says picking from the rack, that is the order picker. Pallet jacks you ride are walkie riders. Call the employer or read the posting closely before spending the money.

Questions worth asking any school

  1. Will I actually operate the truck, and for how long?
  2. Is the practical evaluation included in the price?
  3. What does the card say, and which truck type is it for?
  4. What happens if I do not pass the first time?
  5. Can I renew here when the three years are up?

That last one matters because employers in Ontario commonly re-evaluate operators about every three years, following the CSA B335 standard. That is industry practice, not a provincial statute, and we cover it in a separate article on renewals.

Why the cheapest option is not always the cheapest

A theory-only course can look like a bargain, but an employer in Scarborough, Markham or Pickering will usually want to know you have driven the truck. A card that records classroom time only may get you nothing at the interview, and you pay twice when you go back for the practical. When you compare prices, compare what is included: seat time on the machine, an evaluation, and a card with the truck type on it. Reviewers of A.RSM Training Centre often mention hands-on time and patient instructors, which is what you want to hear from any school before you pay.

If you are paying yourself, one more practical point: ask whether you can bring your own steel-toe boots and a hi-vis vest, and what the school expects you to wear. Show up dressed for a work floor, not an office. The old site also lists safety helmets and boots for sale, which may help if you arrive without them, though you should confirm availability by phone first.

What to do next

Decide which truck you will be driving, ask the all-in price, and book a time. You can reach A.RSM Training Centre at (416) 297-5435 or use the booking form on this site. Bring government photo ID, and if you are renewing, bring your old card.

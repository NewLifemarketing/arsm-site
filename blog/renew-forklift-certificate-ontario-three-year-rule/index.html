How Often Do You Renew a Forklift Certificate in Ontario? The Three-Year Rule Explained

Is there a legal three-year forklift renewal in Ontario? Not in the way most people think. Here is what the regulation says and what employers actually do.

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Forklift Certificate Renewal Ontario

By A.RSM Training Centre · September 21, 2026 · 6 min read

How often do you renew a forklift certificate in Ontario? Ask around a Toronto or Etobicoke warehouse and you will hear “every three years” from almost everyone. They are right about the practice. They are usually wrong about where the rule comes from, and that difference matters when an inspector asks a question or when a new employer looks at your card.

Where the three-year rule comes from

The three-year interval is industry practice. It follows CSA B335, the Canadian standard for lift truck operator training, which is a voluntary standard. It recommends that operators be re-evaluated at least once every three years. Employers adopt it because it is the recognized benchmark, and because it gives them a defensible answer if something goes wrong.

It is not written into an Ontario statute or regulation as a fixed expiry date. If someone tells you the law says your card dies on the third anniversary, they are simplifying. The law works differently, and it is worth understanding how.

What the Ontario regulation actually says

Regulation 851 (Industrial Establishments) does not mention forklifts by name and does not require a forklift licence. What it says is that lifting devices and mobile equipment are to be operated only by a competent person, or by a worker who is being instructed and accompanied by a competent person. Sections 51 and 54 are the ones to read. Section 52 adds safeguards when a lift truck is used to raise workers. On construction projects, Regulation 213/91 section 96 says no worker shall operate a vehicle or powered machine unless he or she is competent to do so.

The Occupational Health and Safety Act, section 25, puts the duty on the employer to give workers information, instruction and supervision. The Ministry of Labour's guidance on powered lift trucks says the employer must establish that operators are competent and may issue certificates.

In Ontario, competence is the legal test. The three-year card is how most employers prove it.

So does my card expire?

That depends on whose card it is and who is asking. A certificate issued by a training school does not carry a legal expiry set by government. What it carries is the date of training and, usually, the school's or the employer's stated re-evaluation period. Most employers treat three years as the outer limit. Many will not accept a card older than that, and some ask for less.

We have not confirmed a certificate validity period for A.RSM Training Centre's cards, so if you need to know what date your employer will accept, ask the employer. If your employer follows CSA B335, plan on re-evaluation at the three-year mark.

When you should be re-evaluated sooner

The three-year mark is a ceiling, not a floor. There are situations where a good employer will re-evaluate an operator earlier:

  • After an incident or near miss involving the operator.
  • When the operator is seen driving unsafely.
  • When the operator moves to a different type of truck, for example from a counterbalance to a reach truck.
  • When the workplace changes in a way that affects the work: new racking, new pedestrian routes, different loads or attachments.
  • After a long absence from driving.

Training for a different truck is not a renewal

A common mix-up in Etobicoke and Mississauga distribution centres: a worker with a counterbalance card gets moved to a reach truck and assumes the card covers it. It does not. Each truck type has its own controls and its own hazards. Reviewers of A.RSM mention taking reach truck training over two days, which shows how much more there is to it than a top-up. If the truck changes, book the course for the new truck.

What a renewal looks like

Expect a review of theory, a check that you understand the current rules and your truck's load ratings, and a practical evaluation on the machine. Several Google reviewers mention renewing with A.RSM and finishing the same day. Timing depends on you and the group, so ask when you book. Bring your old card, government photo ID, and a note of which truck type you need.

What employers should keep

If you are the employer, keep a record for each operator: name, truck type, training date, who evaluated them, and the site orientation you gave. Put the next evaluation date in a calendar, not in someone's head. If an inspector shows up after an incident, that paper is the first thing they ask for.

What to say if an inspector or auditor asks

Do not say “the law requires a card every three years.” Say what is true: the employer is responsible for establishing that each operator is competent, the company follows CSA B335 as its benchmark, operators are re-evaluated at least every three years, and here is the record. That answer is accurate, it shows a system rather than a habit, and it survives follow-up questions. If your company has no written policy, write one page. Name the standard you follow, the re-evaluation interval, who does the evaluation, and where the records live.

Workers should keep their own copy of every card and training date, because employers change, systems get lost, and a photo of a card from 2022 can save a day of retraining when you start somewhere new.

What to do next

Look at the date on your card. If it is two and a half years old or more, book now rather than the week it expires. To arrange a renewal at the Scarborough centre, call (416) 297-5435 and mention which truck type you drive.

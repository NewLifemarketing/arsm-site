An aerial work platform, or AWP, lifts a worker in a guarded basket to a task at height. A scissor lift raises a flat platform straight up on crossing arms; boom lifts articulate and reach out. Our old-site description covers self-propelled elevated work platforms and teaching operators to recognise and control the hazards. Which type you use depends on the task, and each has its own limits.

These machines are safer than ladders for many jobs, but they fail in predictable ways. They tip on slopes, soft ground or when driven elevated over a bump. Workers are thrown or crushed against overhead structures. Contact with power lines is deadly. Falls happen at the guardrail when someone climbs it or leans out, and when a worker is ejected by a sudden stop or tip. Most incidents are foreseeable.

The course covers machine types, controls, the pre-use inspection, ground and slope assessment, overhead hazards and power line clearances, wind and weather limits, and the rules for the platform: staying inside the rails, carrying tools and material, and what to do in an emergency. Regulation 213/91 s.107 forbids working from a platform supported by a forklift, so we cover why a forklift cage is not a lift. Ground and weather checks come before every lift.

Regulation 213/91 s.147 requires instruction and training on the class of platform for construction work. From 1 January 2027, O. Reg. 117/26 applies to construction projects and asks for both theory and practical training, familiarization with the make and model, and five-year validity. That is a lot of change for a short list of facts, and we would rather flag it than pretend it is settled. Ask your employer or the constructor on site which version applies to your work.

What you will learn

  • Types of platform: scissor lifts, boom lifts and what each does well
  • Pre-use inspection: guardrails, gates, controls, tires, hydraulics, emergency lowering and outriggers
  • Ground conditions: slopes, soft ground, holes and edges
  • Load and occupant limits, and how tools and materials count against them
  • Power line clearance distances and overhead obstructions
  • Wind and weather limits, particularly outdoors
  • Fall protection and platform rules: guardrails, harness where required, no climbing
  • Driving the platform elevated versus lowered, and stopping smoothly
  • Emergency lowering, rescue planning and what to do after a tip or trip
  • Parking, charging and securing the machine

Who this course is for

  • Maintenance staff changing lights or servicing overhead equipment
  • Trades on construction and renovation sites
  • Warehouse and retail crews working on high shelving or signage
  • Rental customers who want training before they take a machine off the lot
  • Employers preparing for the January 2027 construction requirements

Scissor Lift & AWP — common questions

Is this course accepted on Ontario construction sites?

Regulation 213/91 s.147 requires instruction and training on the class of platform. From 1 January 2027, O. Reg. 117/26 will require theory plus practical training, make and model familiarization, and five-year validity on construction projects. Whether this course alone meets that depends on content and familiarization.

Does the certificate expire?

Under O. Reg. 117/26, from 1 January 2027, training on construction projects is valid for five years. Before then, and outside construction, no fixed expiry exists in Ontario; employers decide. Ask your employer what they accept and record the date on your certificate so you know when to renew.

Can I use a forklift with a cage to reach high spots?

No. Regulation 213/91 s.107 prohibits working from a platform supported by a forklift, and Regulation 851 s.52 allows a lift truck to raise workers only with safeguards. An aerial work platform is designed for the job, with guardrails and controls at the platform. That is why this is a separate course.

Do I need fall protection training as well?

It depends on the platform and your employer's rules. Some lifts require a harness tied to an anchor point; scissor lifts with full guardrails often do not, but the employer decides. Fall Protection is a separate $100 course covering harness use if you need it.

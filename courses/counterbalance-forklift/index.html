A counterbalance forklift is the machine most people picture: forks on the front, a heavy weight over the rear axle, and an operator seated between. That rear weight is what lets the truck lift a pallet without falling forward. It also means the truck steers with its back wheels, swings its tail wide in a turn, and depends on a narrow triangle of stability that is easy to leave without noticing.

Most forklift injuries come from a short list of causes: tip-overs on turns or ramps, pedestrians struck in blind spots, loads that fall from a raised mast, and operators who step off a tipping truck instead of staying belted in. None of these need bad luck. They need speed, a raised load, or a distracted moment, which is why the course spends as much time on judgment as on controls.

The classroom part covers how the truck is built, how to read the data plate, how load weight and load centre change what the truck can safely lift, and how to inspect it before a shift. The practical part puts you on a truck: travelling, turning, picking up, stacking, placing and parking, with an instructor watching how you handle each move. Under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, an employer must make sure a worker operating a lift truck is competent and adequately trained. Ontario does not issue a forklift licence. What you leave with is training and a certificate to show an employer, who decides what else they need from you on their own site.

What you will learn

  • Pre-use inspection: forks, chains, mast, tires, brakes, horn, lights, seat belt and fluid or battery checks
  • Controls and gauges, and how the truck steers with its rear wheels
  • Reading the data plate: rated capacity, load centre and what attachments do to both
  • The stability triangle and why speed, turns and raised loads tip a truck
  • Approaching, lifting, tilting, travelling and placing a palletized load
  • Stacking and retrieving from racking, including checking the rack and the load before you lift
  • Travelling on ramps, slopes and dock plates, and where the load goes on a grade
  • Working around pedestrians: horn use, blind corners, right of way and exclusion zones
  • Propane and battery safety, refuelling and charging
  • Parking, shutdown and what to do if a truck starts to tip

Who this course is for

  • First-time operators heading into warehouse, shipping, retail or manufacturing work
  • Workers whose employer has asked for proof of forklift training
  • Experienced operators who learned on the job and have nothing on paper
  • Supervisors who need to recognise unsafe operation when they see it
  • Employers putting several people through at once (see corporate training)

Counterbalance Forklift — common questions

Do I need a forklift licence in Ontario?

No provincial forklift licence exists. Ontario law requires employers to make sure workers are competent and adequately trained before operating a lift truck. Training from a school like ours gives you a certificate to show an employer, but the employer remains responsible for training on their own equipment and site, and may ask you to do more.

How long is my forklift certificate good for?

There is no fixed Ontario expiry. Industry practice, based on the voluntary CSA B335 standard, is to re-evaluate operators about every three years, and sooner after an incident or a change of equipment. Your employer decides what they accept. Ask them before you book so you do not pay twice.

I have never driven a forklift. Is that a problem?

No. The course starts from the controls and works up to placing a load on racking. Most people find the first hour on the truck awkward because of the rear-wheel steering, then settle in. If you are nervous, tell the instructor at the start so they pace it properly.

Will this cover the truck at my workplace?

It covers a sit-down counterbalance truck. If your job uses a reach truck, order picker or walkie rider, take that course instead, since they handle differently. Your employer should also give you familiarization on their specific truck, attachments and site hazards.

Falls from height are among the leading causes of serious injury and death in Ontario workplaces. A fall protection system is only as good as its weakest link: the anchor, the connector, the lanyard, the harness and the person wearing it. A harness worn loosely, a lanyard clipped to a guardrail, or an anchor that cannot take the load can fail exactly when it is needed. Most falls involve a person and a routine task.

Our course covers the hazards of working at heights, the hierarchy of protection (eliminate the hazard, guard it, restrain the worker, arrest the fall), how to inspect and put on a harness, how to choose and check an anchor point, and how fall distance, swing and suspension trauma change a rescue. The old site describes it as covering the hazards of working at heights.

Important distinction: this is not the Working at Heights program Ontario requires for many construction workers. Under O. Reg. 297/13, construction workers need Chief Prevention Officer-approved WAH training from an approved provider, valid for three years. A.RSM's course is a general fall protection course at $100 and is not that program. If your job is on a construction project, check your employer's requirement before you book. If it is a warehouse, roof access, or maintenance task outside construction, this course may be what your employer wants. Either way, employers must ensure workers are trained on the fall protection they use.

What you will learn

  • Common fall hazards: edges, openings, ladders, scaffolds and roofs
  • The hierarchy of fall protection, from elimination to arrest
  • Types of harness and how to fit one properly
  • Pre-use inspection of harnesses, lanyards, lifelines and connectors
  • Anchor points: what qualifies, where to place them, and capacity
  • Shock-absorbing lanyards, self-retracting lifelines and their limits
  • Fall clearance and swing-fall calculations
  • Suspension trauma and why a rescue plan matters
  • Ladder safety and three-point contact
  • Documenting inspections and removing damaged equipment from service

Who this course is for

  • Maintenance and facilities workers using roof access or elevated platforms
  • Trades doing occasional work at height
  • Warehouse workers whose equipment requires a harness (for example some order pickers)
  • Employers building a broader safety training record

Fall Protection — common questions

Is this the Ministry-approved Working at Heights course?

No. Ontario's Working at Heights program under O. Reg. 297/13 must be delivered by a provider approved by the Chief Prevention Officer, and it is valid for three years. This $100 fall protection course is not that program. If you work on a construction project, check your employer's requirement before you book.

Who needs approved Working at Heights training?

Generally, workers on construction projects who may use a fall protection system. The exact requirement is set by Regulation 213/91 and O. Reg. 297/13 and depends on the work. Your employer or the site's constructor can tell you. If they say WAH, book an approved provider; if they say fall protection awareness, this course may fit.

Does the certificate expire?

There is no fixed Ontario expiry for a general fall protection course. Employers often re-train periodically and after any incident or change in equipment. The approved WAH program is valid for three years, but that is a separate program. Ask your employer what they accept.

Do I need my own harness?

You do not need one to attend. Your employer supplies the equipment you use at work and must make sure it is suitable and inspected. For the class, we use training equipment.

A walkie rider is a powered pallet truck. The operator either walks beside it or steps onto a small platform at the rear and rides it through the warehouse. It moves pallets short distances at ground level, at docks, in receiving and staging areas, and in retail back rooms. Because it looks small and simple, it is often given to new workers with little instruction. You will find them in nearly every dock and stockroom.

That is where the injuries come from. Feet and legs get crushed between the truck and a wall, rack or dock edge. Loads shift on a hard stop. Trucks run off dock edges or down ramps, and people are struck at blind corners because a rider is moving faster than someone walking. A loaded powered truck weighs enough that a small mistake becomes a serious one. Speed makes it worse, since a rider covers ground quickly.

The course covers how the truck is built, how its controls, brakes and speed limiter work, how to read capacity and load balance, and how to inspect it. In the practical you drive the truck through aisles, pick up and set down pallets, work a ramp and a dock plate, and practise stopping distances with and without a load. There is no separate Ontario walkie rider licence. Employers must ensure workers are competent and adequately trained on the equipment they use. Our course gives you formal training and a certificate to show your employer, who decides what else is required on their site.

What you will learn

  • Pre-use inspection: forks, wheels, brakes, steering handle, horn, battery and operator platform
  • Controls and the difference between walk-behind and stand-on travel
  • Capacity plates and load balance on pallets
  • Safe operator position and keeping feet clear of the truck
  • Braking distance and speed control with and without a load
  • Picking up and setting down pallets, including damaged or unstable ones
  • Ramps, dock plates and trailer entry
  • Pedestrian awareness, horns and blind-corner rules
  • Battery charging and safe handling
  • Parking, shutdown and reporting faults

Who this course is for

  • Dock, receiving and shipping workers
  • Retail and grocery staff who move pallets in back rooms
  • New hires who have been told to "just use the pallet jack"
  • Employers wanting written training for their powered pallet trucks

Walkie Rider — common questions

Do I need this if I only use a manual pallet jack?

No. A manual pallet jack has no motor and no operator training standard behind it, although injuries still happen. This course is for powered walkie and walkie rider trucks. If your workplace has both, your employer should still instruct you on safe handling of the manual jack.

Is a walkie rider covered by a forklift certificate?

Not automatically. The controls, stopping behaviour and operator position are different, and Ontario law asks employers to make sure a worker is competent on the equipment they actually use. Many employers accept a forklift course plus familiarization; others want walkie rider training specifically. Ask yours.

How long is the certificate valid?

No Ontario statute sets an expiry. Industry practice, based on the voluntary CSA B335 standard, is about three years between re-evaluations, and sooner after an incident or a change of equipment. Your employer decides what they accept. Keep the date on your card so you know when to renew.

What should I wear?

Closed-toe, sturdy footwear is the minimum, since feet are the main casualty on these trucks; steel-toe boots are the safe choice. Our safety products page lists boots if you need them.

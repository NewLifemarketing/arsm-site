WHMIS, the Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System, is Canada's way of telling workers what is in the products they use and how those products can hurt them. Since 2015 it follows the Globally Harmonized System, which is why the labels and safety data sheets carry the same pictograms and sections in Canada as in many other countries. That means a worker at a warehouse in Scarborough reads the same skull-and-crossbones as one anywhere else in Canada.

The hazards are ordinary and easy to underestimate: a cleaning product decanted into an unmarked bottle, solvent fumes in a poorly ventilated room, a pressurized cylinder stored beside heat, or two chemicals that react when they meet. Most WHMIS incidents are not dramatic; they are unread labels, unavailable data sheets and workers who were never told what the product does. A short course cannot fix that, but it does teach you to stop and read the label before you pour, mix or store anything.

The course covers what WHMIS is and why it exists, the hazard classes and their pictograms, how to read supplier and workplace labels, how to find and read the sixteen sections of a safety data sheet, and the rules for storage, handling and emergencies. It is generic training, which brings an important limit. Ontario's Regulation 860 requires employers to instruct workers on labels, safety data sheets and safe handling, and to provide training specific to the products and procedures in that workplace. A general course like ours is awareness training. Your employer must still cover the chemicals, storage areas and emergency procedures at your site, which is why we do not sell this as a stand-alone answer.

WHMIS 2015 — common questions

Is a general WHMIS course enough for my job?

Usually not on its own. Ontario's Regulation 860 requires employers to give instruction on labels, data sheets and safe handling, and training specific to the hazardous products and procedures at your workplace. A general course is awareness training that covers the first part. Your employer must provide the site-specific part.

Does WHMIS training expire?

Ontario's Regulation 860 sets no expiry period. Employers decide when to refresh, and commonly do so when new products arrive, when procedures change or after an incident. Some sites ask for annual refreshers. Ask your employer what they need and keep your certificate date.

What is different about WHMIS 2015?

It aligns Canada with the Globally Harmonized System. Labels use standard pictograms and signal words, and safety data sheets follow a sixteen-section format. If you learned the old WHMIS 1988 system, the symbols are different and you should retrain. Ask your employer if they want a refresh.

Do I need this if I only use household cleaners at work?

Perhaps. WHMIS applies to hazardous products used in workplaces, including many cleaning products sold in commercial packaging. If your employer supplies labels and data sheets for them, you should be trained. If unsure, ask your supervisor. Your employer knows which products are on site.

Costs and booking

How much do the courses cost?

Prices are listed for every course. Counterbalance, reach truck, order picker and walkie rider forklift courses are $150 each. Skid-steer is $350. Aerial work platform and scissor lift is $200. Fall protection is $100. WHMIS 2015 and TDG are $49 each. Most GTA schools do not publish prices at all, so we do.

Are there any extra fees?

Some of our course pages mention a $75 fee for practical training and for creating and printing the wallet card. We have not confirmed that it applies to every course, so the listed price may not be the full amount.

How do I book?

Call the office at (416) 297-5435 or email info@arsmtrainingcentre.com, and tell us which course you want. We will find you a time. Ask about rescheduling when you book.

Where are you?

A.RSM Training Centre is at 60 Nugget Avenue, Unit 13, Scarborough, Ontario. The phone number is (416) 297-5435. Please phone before you drive over, so we can confirm that a class is running and that someone is there to meet you.

Is the centre open on weekends?

Weekday hours of 9:30 to 5:30 are the only hours all our sources agree on. Our older site said we are open six days a week, with a seventh day by pre-booked appointment. Call (416) 297-5435 to confirm a weekend time.

How long does a course take?

It depends on the course. Some Google reviewers report finishing forklift training and getting their card the same day, in two to three hours. One reviewer took reach truck training over two days. Tell us your experience when you call and we will give you a realistic time.

What should I bring, and how do I pay?

We have not confirmed an official list, so call the office at (416) 297-5435 before you come in. Practical training happens on real machines, so plan for sturdy footwear. We also sell safety boots ($50) and helmets ($30) if you need them at the centre.

Forklift certification

Do I need experience to take a forklift course?

No. Nothing we have found requires previous experience. The course covers theory and then practical time on the machine, and reviewers describe patient instructors and plenty of hands-on time. If you already operate a forklift, tell us so we can focus on evaluation and gaps.

Does the law require a forklift licence?

Not by that name. Ontario's Regulation 851 never uses the word forklift or asks for a licence. It requires that lifting devices and mobile equipment be operated by a competent person. Employers must instruct and supervise workers, and most establish competence with a training certificate. That is why most employers ask for one.

What do I get when I finish?

You get a certificate and a wallet card showing the equipment class you trained on. Several reviewers say they left with it the same day. Your employer still has to be satisfied you are competent on their machines and site, so expect a short workplace orientation at any job.

How long does forklift certification last?

Ontario law sets no expiry. Re-evaluating operators every three years is industry practice, tied to the voluntary CSA B335 standard, and many employers follow it. Some also retrain after an incident or a change of equipment. Ask your employer what it wants. We also do renewals; reviewers mention them.

Which forklift course should I take?

Match the course to the machine you will use. Counterbalance is the classic sit-down forklift. Reach truck is for narrow warehouse aisles. Order picker lifts the operator with the load. Walkie rider is a low-lift pallet truck you stand on. All four are $150. If a job posting names the machine, take that one.

Can I take the course if English is my second language?

Yes. Our older site says instruction is offered in English, Punjabi, Mandarin, Cantonese, Hindi and Tamil, and reviewers mention that all languages are available. Ask for your language when you book, because it depends on which instructor is in that day.

Is there a minimum age?

Ontario restricts young workers from operating some equipment, and employers set their own rules on top of that. Call us before you book if you are under 18 so we can tell you honestly whether the course will be of use to you.

Do you offer forklift renewals or refresher training?

Yes. Renewals appear in our Google reviews and are a routine reason people come back, often at the three-year mark many employers use. A renewal is a shorter re-evaluation than a first course. Bring your old card if you still have it.

Lifts, skid-steers and heavy equipment

What does the skid-steer course cover, and what does it cost?

Skid-steer training is $350. It covers the controls, which make the machine useful and also dangerous, plus hands-on operation. Reviewers mention Bobcat-style training with us. It is priced above the forklift courses, and the price is listed openly.

Is a skid-steer licence required in Ontario?

No Ontario regulation names a skid-steer licence. The rules that apply are the competence requirements: Regulation 851 section 54 for industrial work, and Regulation 213/91 section 96 on construction projects. Employers want proof of training, and our certificate is that proof for your file.

What is the aerial work platform and scissor lift course?

It costs $200 and covers recognising and controlling hazards on self-propelled elevated work platforms. On construction projects, Regulation 213/91 section 147 requires instruction on the class of platform you use. Ask us which platform types the practical uses.

Is there anything I should know about the new elevating work platform rule?

Ontario Regulation 117/26 takes effect on 1 January 2027 for construction projects. It requires theory and practical training, familiarization with the make and model, and a training that stays valid for five years. Confirm with us how our course lines up with it before you rely on it for a construction job.

Can a forklift lift me up to work at height?

Not casually. Regulation 213/91 section 107 says nobody may work from a platform supported by a forklift on a construction project. Lift trucks that raise workers in other settings need safeguards under Regulation 851 section 52. If you need to reach something high, use a proper lift platform, and train on it.

Workplace safety: WHMIS, TDG and fall protection

What is WHMIS, and who needs it?

WHMIS 2015 is Canada's hazard communication system, covering labels and safety data sheets under the GHS. It costs $49. Any worker exposed to hazardous products must be trained. A general course like ours gives awareness only. Ontario also expects your employer to teach the specific hazards, labels and procedures of your own workplace.

Does my WHMIS certificate expire?

Ontario's Regulation 860 sets no expiry period for WHMIS training. Many employers ask for a refresher every one to three years anyway, and you should retrain whenever your workplace introduces new hazardous products. Ask your employer what it wants.

Who needs TDG training?

Anyone who handles, offers for transport or transports dangerous goods. It costs $49. Under the federal Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations, the employer issues the training certificate, not the school. Ours is proof you completed the training. Keep your records, since your employer must too.

How long is TDG training valid?

The certificate an employer issues expires after 36 months for road and rail, and 24 months for air. Employers keep records for two years after expiry. Retrain at or before those points, or sooner when the regulations change or your job changes. Ask us which mode of transport your job involves.

Is your Fall Protection course the same as Working at Heights?

No. Our $100 Fall Protection course covers the hazards of working at heights. It is not the Ministry-approved Working at Heights program, which construction workers need from an approved provider and which is valid for three years. If your site needs that program, book it elsewhere first.

So when is the $100 fall protection course useful?

It is useful as a general introduction to fall hazards, for workers outside construction, and as supporting training your employer builds on with site-specific instruction. It is not a substitute for approved Working at Heights training. When unsure, ask your employer or the contractor which one is required.

Can one course cover WHMIS, TDG and fall protection together?

They are separate courses with separate prices: WHMIS $49, TDG $49, fall protection $100. Ask about doing more than one on the same visit.

Employers and groups

Do you offer group rates?

We have not published group pricing, and we will not invent a discount here. For a team, call (416) 297-5435 and tell us how many people need training, which courses they need and when you would like to start. We will quote you directly.

Can you train my staff at our workplace?

One reviewer describes onsite training for a company, so it has been done. Onsite training also lets us check your own machines and aisles. Call with your location, equipment and headcount.

What does Ontario expect from employers on forklift training?

The Occupational Health and Safety Act, section 25(2)(a), requires information, instruction and supervision, and section 25(2)(c) a competent supervisor. Operators must be competent persons. A certificate helps, but you must also train on your own machines and site and keep records.

Do you give employers records for their files?

Every trainee receives a certificate and wallet card. Employers should keep a copy on file. For TDG, remember it is the employer who issues the certificate, and it must keep records for two years after expiry.

Can you replace a lost certificate or card?

Call the office at (416) 297-5435 with your name, the course you took and the approximate date, and we will check whether we can find your record. If you are unsure which course or equipment class you were trained on, say so. Some employers also keep a copy in your personnel file, so ask them too.

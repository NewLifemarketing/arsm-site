East York is mostly homes and small businesses, with a working industrial pocket in Leaside. Our Scarborough centre is a short drive out the Danforth or the Don Valley.

East York is smaller and more residential than the rest of Toronto's core, but the working industry that exists there is real. Leaside Business Park along Laird Drive and Eglinton Avenue is the largest concentration, with distribution, printing, light manufacturing and trade suppliers in mid-sized buildings. Thorncliffe Park, near the Don Valley, has offices, retail and some light industrial space. Along the Danforth and Overlea Boulevard, contractors, building suppliers and small warehouses operate out of older properties with tight loading areas. Many of these firms are small, with five to twenty staff, and one forklift often serves the whole operation. Loading happens at curbside docks and shared laneways in many cases.

Because the area is close to the Don Valley Parkway and the Danforth, the trip to our Scarborough centre is one of the shorter ones from central Toronto. Expect about 20 to 30 minutes in normal traffic by taking the DVP to Hwy 401 east, then Markham Road and Sheppard Avenue East. If you would rather stay off highways, Victoria Park Avenue north to Sheppard is a workable option, though traffic lights slow it down. Most East York workers will find a same-day course quite manageable without a long commute.

The most common training need in East York comes from renovation and construction work. Homes here are older, and contractors doing additions, basement underpinning and rebuilds frequently need scissor lift or aerial work platform training, fall protection, and sometimes a skid-steer for site clearing or grading. The Leaside distributors and printers lean toward counterbalance forklift certification, with reach truck and order picker for anyone working racking. WHMIS 2015 is worth having for anyone using cleaning products, adhesives or paints, and TDG suits shipping and receiving staff. Trades working in older East York houses often add fall protection to lift work for the same reason.

Ontario employers, including small ones, must ensure workers are adequately trained and supervised for the equipment they use. For an East York contractor with two or three employees, that duty is as real as it is for a large plant, and the paperwork should show it. Many businesses here are small enough that one person books a course for the whole team. If you have a few people to train, phone (416) 297-5435 and ask about a group. On-site training at your workplace is worth asking about, though we cannot confirm it in advance.

Training from East York: questions

How far is A.RSM from East York?

It is roughly 20 to 30 minutes by car in normal traffic. Most people take the Don Valley Parkway to Hwy 401 east, then Markham Road and Sheppard Avenue East to Nugget Ave, though Victoria Park Avenue north also works. Check traffic before leaving during rush hour.

Do East York employers need forklift certification?

Ontario employers must ensure workers are adequately trained for the equipment they use, forklifts included. East York has warehouses and printers in Leaside as well as contractors with lifts and skid-steers. If you are not sure what applies to your job, ask your employer, and call (416) 297-5435 to pick the right course.

Can a group from East York train together?

You can ask us about it. Small East York businesses sometimes send two or three workers together, for example a renovation crew that needs scissor lift and fall protection. Call (416) 297-5435 to discuss numbers. If you want training at your own workplace, ask about that too; it is not promised until confirmed.
